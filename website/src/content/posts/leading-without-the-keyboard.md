The fastest way to fix a problem on a team is for the most experienced engineer to fix it. It is also the fastest way to make sure the team never learns to fix it themselves.

## The keyboard is a bottleneck

When I moved into a lead role, my instinct during an incident or a stuck pull request was to take over. It felt responsible. In practice it meant every hard problem routed through one person, and that person became the limit on how much the team could take on.

What changed my approach was treating my own time as the scarcest resource in the system — and asking what it was best spent on.

## What I do instead

- **Ask for the hypothesis first.** "What do you think is happening?" gets someone reasoning instead of waiting.
- **Pair, but navigate.** I describe direction; they drive. It is slower the first time and much faster the fifth.
- **Write the decision down.** A short architecture decision record turns a one-off conversation into something the next person can read.
- **Make review a teaching surface.** A review comment that explains _why_ is worth ten that just say what to change.

## When to take the keyboard anyway

There are moments where speed matters more than growth — a production outage with customers waiting is one of them. The skill is noticing which moment you are in, and saying so out loud: "I am going to drive this one because of the impact; let us walk through it together afterwards."

That last part is the one that matters. The fix gets the system back. The walkthrough is what gets the team back.
